# Non-Quantitative & Qualitative Academic Disciplines
**Date**: August 5, 2026  
**File**: `docs/_non_terra_sciences.md`  
**Purpose**: Comprehensive academic overview of qualitative, clinical, normative, and hermeneutic disciplines, exploring their unique methodologies, epistemologies, and modes of inquiry.

---

## 🏛️ 1. The Humanities & Qualitative Social Sciences

### Disciplines:
History, Sociology, Cultural Anthropology, Political Philosophy, Literary Theory, Comparative Religion, and Qualitative Linguistics.

### Epistemology & Methodology:
- **Hermeneutics & Textual Analysis**: Interpreting historical documents, literary works, and cultural texts within their specific temporal and socio-political contexts.
- **Ethnography & Participant Observation**: Immersing researchers within human communities to observe cultural rituals, social hierarchies, and lived experiences from an insider (*emic*) perspective.
- **Historiography & Archival Research**: Evaluating primary source documents, oral histories, and artifacts to reconstruct past events and trace institutional evolution.
- **Discourse Analysis**: Examining how language, ideology, and power structures shape social norms, identities, and institutions.

### Core Inquiry:
These fields investigate **human meaning, agency, culture, and power**. Rather than seeking universal, time-invariant mathematical laws, they explore how human societies construct value systems, navigate historical contingencies, organize institutions, and express collective identities.

## 🩺 2. Clinical Medicine & Health Sciences

### Disciplines:
Clinical Diagnostics, Surgical Medicine, Gross Anatomy, Clinical Pathology, Nursing, Physical Therapy, and Medical Ethics.

### Epistemology & Methodology:
- **Clinical Observation & Physical Examination**: Assessing physical symptoms, vital signs, organ palpation, and patient history to diagnose illness.
- **Anatomical Dissection & Structural Mapping**: Studying the macro and microscopic physical architecture of human organs, tissues, and skeletal systems.
- **Surgical Procedure & Triage**: Applying manual dexterity, anatomical knowledge, and real-time procedural decision-making under acute physiological conditions.
- **Diagnostic Protocols & Differential Diagnosis**: Systematic elimination of disease candidates based on clinical presentation, lab biomarkers, and medical imaging.

### Core Inquiry:
These disciplines focus on **healing, physical intervention, and pathology in individual biological organisms**. The primary goal is procedural and diagnostic efficacy—restoring health, mitigating suffering, and repairing anatomical structures—rather than deriving generalized mathematical field equations.

## 🧠 3. Clinical & Qualitative Psychology

### Disciplines:
Clinical Psychology, Psychotherapy, Psychoanalysis, Developmental Psychology, and Social & Personality Psychology.

### Epistemology & Methodology:
- **Therapeutic Modalities**: Utilizing verbal dialogue, cognitive restructuring (CBT), psychodynamic exploration, and mindfulness techniques to treat mental health disorders.
- **Qualitative Case Studies & In-Depth Interviews**: Analyzing rich, individual narrative accounts of trauma, identity, emotional growth, and interpersonal relationships.
- **Observational Behavioral Coding**: Monitoring human social interactions, child developmental milestones (e.g., Piagetian stages), and non-verbal communication in naturalistic environments.
- **Psychometric Assessment**: Standardizing diagnostic criteria (e.g., DSM-5) to classify psychological states, personality traits, and emotional well-being.

### Core Inquiry:
These fields explore **subjective human experience, emotional health, personal identity, and interpersonal dynamics**. They seek to understand mental states, therapeutic healing, self-awareness, and the human condition as experienced from within.

## 📜 4. Pure Logic, Metalogic & Formal Philosophy

### Disciplines:
Epistemology, Moral Philosophy (Ethics), Metaphysics, Modal Logic, Philosophy of Language, and Political Theory.

### Epistemology & Methodology:
- **Conceptual Analysis**: Deconstructing complex ideas (e.g., *justice*, *knowledge*, *truth*, *causality*) into their necessary and sufficient logical components.
- **Thought Experiments & Counterfactual Reasoning**: Constructing hypothetical scenarios (e.g., the *Trolley Problem*, *Gettier Cases*, *Searle's Chinese Room*) to test the boundary limits of ethical theories and cognitive claims.
- **Normative Argumentation**: Deriving claims about what *ought* to be (deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics) rather than describing empirical facts about what *is*.
- **Deductive & Modal Logic**: Evaluating the formal validity of propositional inferences, necessary truths, and possible-world semantics using symbolic notation.

### Core Inquiry:
These disciplines examine **normative values, the nature of knowledge, truth criteria, and ethical obligation**. They evaluate how rational beings should reason, what constitutes a justified belief, and how moral principles ought to guide human action.
