                    <h3>1. Foundation: D'Alembert's Principle and Virtual Work</h3>
                    <p>The physical basis of <a href="/physics/subtopic/lagrangian" class="subtopic-link"><strong>Lagrangian</strong></a> mechanics is the <a href="/physics/subtopic/principle-of-virtual-work" class="subtopic-link"><strong>Principle of Virtual Work</strong></a>. For a system in equilibrium, the work done by applied forces during an infinitesimal, constraint-consistent displacement (\( \delta \mathbf{r} \)) is zero. <a href="/physics/subtopic/dalemberts-principle" class="subtopic-link"><strong>D'Alembert's Principle</strong></a> extends this to dynamic systems by introducing "inertial forces" (\( -m\mathbf{a} \)):
                    \[ \sum_i (\mathbf{F}_i - \dot{\mathbf{p}}_i) \cdot \delta \mathbf{r}_i = 0 \]
                    By assuming that constraint forces (like the tension in a rigid rod) perform no virtual work, this principle allows us to eliminate the unknown forces of constraint from the equations of motion entirely.</p>
